## Witness statement

> I believe the system's intent was to help people who could not get medical help in their own country, and that this evolved into a broader idea that America would help.
>
> My Minnesota perspective is that we are nice here, and that “nice” can hide a lot of complexity. I see a history in which doctors, policies, and state systems tried to help global patients get medical care. When that care took months or years, I believe patients could remain inside benefit structures tied to their medical status.
>
> My concern is not that “collision” is itself an explanation. Collision is only where systems touch. The harder question is what survives when healthcare, immigration or public-benefit systems, courts, technology, AI, and government information systems all interact.
>
> AI makes the provenance question unavoidable. What is the machine actually reasoning from? Is it ordinary public text, government PDFs, congressional or agency material, platform content, or a genuinely replayable governance system with cryptographic provenance and append-only records?
>
> I want the system to distinguish between documents that merely look official and records whose origin, version, authority, and history can actually be replayed.
>
> I also want the system to preserve the human stakes. Patients still exist. The medical industry still exists. Judges, voters, taxpayers, people asking for answers, and AI systems are now colliding with one another. If ordinary people cannot understand where authority came from, what changed, what evidence was used, and how to challenge it, then the operating picture has failed them.
>
> My question is therefore not simply “who collided with whom?” It is: **What is the receipt, whose authority is being exercised, who benefits, who bears the cost, and can an ordinary person replay the decision?**
