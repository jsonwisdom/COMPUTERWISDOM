## Claim
Public fundraising/teaching language links Passover/Easter giving with release or receipt of supernatural blessings, creating a live internal-consistency tension under ChristianAudit v0.3.

## Text rails
- Acts 8:18–20 — gift of God not obtainable with money — CONSENSUS principle.
- 2 Corinthians 9:7 — voluntary giving, not under compulsion — CONSENSUS principle.
- Matthew 6:24 — CONSENSUS text; application to fundraising is CONTEXT_DEPENDENT.

## Disputed-text protocol
SUBJECT_FRAMEWORK = prosperity / seed-faith; giving framed as a faith act that may position the giver to receive divine return.

COUNTERINTERPRETATIONS = PRESERVED:
1. Gift is framed as faith/obedience, not purchase.
2. Subject disclaimer: “You're not doing this to get something, but you're doing it in honor to God.”
3. Lower donation amounts were reportedly encouraged for people unable to meet a specific seed amount.
4. Promotional imperative language (“Don't miss your moment to release seven supernatural blessings...”) is preserved alongside the disclaimer; the machine does not resolve the theological conflict.

MACHINE_THEOLOGY_VERDICT = PROHIBITED

## Falsification
MOVES_TO_MATCH_IF:
- fuller primary-source context shows the ministry explicitly teaches that gifts do not purchase, guarantee, trigger, or proportionally increase divine favor;
- supernatural blessings are clearly separated from donation amount;
- the same teaching is freely available without financial threshold;
- language of “release/receive” is demonstrably explained as non-transactional and consistently framed that way.

MOVES_TO_NOT_APPLICABLE_IF:
- the quoted material is inauthentic, materially altered, or not attributable to the subject/ministry;
- the claimed principle being tested was never taught as doctrine or practice;
- the relevant passages are shown, under the subject's stated framework, not to govern the fundraising claim being audited.

MOVES_FROM_TENSION_TO_HOLD_IF:
- direct-source context materially changes the meaning of the excerpts;
- source authenticity or attribution becomes uncertain.
