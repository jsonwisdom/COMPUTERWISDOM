## Purpose

Audit any proposed or actual deployment of an electrically stimulating glove or similar force-producing wearable from the first public dollar through policy authorization, unit assignment, field use, incident reporting, and independent review.

This is an accountability instrument. It does not provide operational instructions for applying the device to a person.

## Federal policy anchor

DHS announced a department-wide Use of Force Policy update in 2023 requiring component agencies to issue policies meeting or exceeding department standards, including requirements involving less-than-lethal force, de-escalation, intervention, training, and use-of-force data collection/reporting.

That department-wide policy anchor does not itself prove that this specific device has been approved, placed on an authorized equipment list, trained, or deployed by ICE.

## Wisconsin transfer

If a Wisconsin law-enforcement agency were to acquire a device meeting Wisconsin's statutory definition of an `electric weapon`, the audit must separately establish the agency's legal possession/use authority and its written use-of-force policy.

Wisconsin Stat. § 66.0511 requires each law-enforcement agency to maintain a written use-of-force policy and make it publicly available, plus a public citizen-complaint procedure.

Wisconsin Stat. § 941.295 defines `electric weapon` broadly as a device designed, used, or intended to immobilize or incapacitate by electric current and contains exceptions including peace officers. Whether a particular shock glove falls within that statutory definition is a device-specific legal classification and must not be assumed from marketing language alone.

Wisconsin DOJ also requires monthly reporting of reportable use-of-force and arrest-related-death incidents by law-enforcement agencies.
