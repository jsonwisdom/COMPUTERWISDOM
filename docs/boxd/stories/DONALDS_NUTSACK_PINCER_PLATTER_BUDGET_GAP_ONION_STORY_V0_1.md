## Receipt basis

This story transforms the figures, evidence states, and source categories supplied in the current BoxD Intelligence Budget ledger. ## ACT II — WHERE THE TRAIL GOES DARK

The Budget Gap Onion has nine layers:

```text
G0 REQUEST
G1 APPROPRIATION
G2 PROGRAM ALLOCATION
G3 REPROGRAMMING / TRANSFER
G4 CONTRACT / VENDOR
G5 EXECUTION
G6 OVERSIGHT
G7 CORRECTIVE ACTION
G8 PUBLIC EXPLANATION
```

At **G2** the public trail crosses the membrane.

That does not mean the money vanished. It means the public accounting resolution drops.

The law can authorize secrecy. Congress can receive classified schedules. Intelligence committees can hold closed briefings. Agencies can lawfully withhold details whose disclosure would expose protected capabilities or operations.

BoxD does not call that corruption.

BoxD calls it:

**PUBLIC RESOLUTION LOSS.**

The public sees the aggregate number but not the full program-level decomposition.

Then the next claim arrives wearing a very American costume: **efficiency**.

ODNI 2.0 says restructuring and workforce reductions will save more than **$700M annually**.

Gray Baby writes one word beside it:

`CLAIMED`

Not rejected. Not mocked. Not proven.

Then the Shock Glove comes out.

> **Cheaper than what?**

What was the baseline cost? What portion was classification overhead? What did secure facilities, cleared staff, contractor security, duplicate classified/unclassified systems, FOIA litigation, declassification review, records segregation, and retention cost?

The supplied public ledger cannot close those equations.

So the savings statement remains **BOUND as an official claim**, while the complete cost-benefit comparison remains **HOLD**.

That is not cynicism.

That is accounting.

## ACT IV — THE GANG-OF-EIGHT UMBRELLA

Closed intelligence briefings create unusual accountability geometry.

The public may know that oversight occurred without having enough detail to independently replay that oversight.

So BoxD gives the oversight layer its own burden:

```text
NOTIFIED != REVIEWED
REVIEWED != CHALLENGED
CHALLENGED != CORRECTED
CLOSED BRIEFING != SECRET GUILT
CLASSIFICATION != CORRUPTION
```

But secrecy is not a substitute for accounting either.

If the public is asked to accept an efficiency argument built inside a classified architecture, the cost of maintaining that architecture becomes a legitimate budget question.

That is the institutional Shock Glove.

## ACT V — THE QUADRATIC GAP

The BoxD quadratic is not a corruption detector. It measures **documentary pressure**.

When the known public topline is large and retrievable program allocation approaches zero because detail is classified, the documentary gap becomes large.

That does not make the spending illegal.

It marks the point where public reproducibility becomes weak.

The governance question is therefore:

> **How much public confidence is being demanded at the exact point where public reproducibility falls away?**

National security can lawfully require secrecy. The democratic answer should still be to conduct the strongest lawful audit of the secrecy architecture itself without publishing protected operational facts.

Audit:

```text
COSTS
CONTROLS
RETENTION

```

Audit the umbrella without publishing what the umbrella lawfully protects.
