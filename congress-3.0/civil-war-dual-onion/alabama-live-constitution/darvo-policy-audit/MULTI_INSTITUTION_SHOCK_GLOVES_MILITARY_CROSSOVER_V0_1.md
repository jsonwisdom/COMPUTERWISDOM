## Executive disposition

The Alabama audit must span multiple institutions. A denial, burden shift, or adverse consequence may originate in one institution and then propagate through another without proving a coordinated DARVO campaign.

The correct unit is the **event edge**:

```text
CLAIM / REPORT
-> INSTITUTION A
-> RULE / AUTHORITY
-> FINDING / DENIAL / ORDER
-> DATA OR NOTICE TRANSFER
-> INSTITUTION B
-> NEW RULE / AUTHORITY
-> NEW CONSEQUENCE
-> REVIEW / APPEAL / RECEIPT
```

A repeated narrative across institutions is an audit signal. It is not proof of common intent unless a binding coordination source exists.

## Shock Gloves definition

`SHOCK_GLOVES` remains an audit metaphor for a state-backed or institution-backed coercive consequence. It does not mean literal electric gloves unless physical-device evidence is bound.

Candidate coercive nodes in Alabama law and policy include:

- custody and visitation orders;
- contempt and court sanctions;
- warrants directing law enforcement to take physical custody of a child in qualifying cases;
- child-support income withholding, liens, levies, passport restrictions, and license suspension;
- SNAP closure/ineligibility for failure to cooperate with a mandatory quality-control review;
- substantiated child-abuse/neglect findings and administrative-review consequences;
- required transmission of child-abuse reports among schools, law enforcement, DHR, and other state agencies;
- military-family notification to a Department of Defense Family Advocacy Program when DHR determines a parent or guardian is military.

These mechanisms are legally distinct. Their existence does not prove misuse.

## Military crossover — source-bound

Alabama Code §26-14-3 requires DHR, after receiving a child-abuse/neglect report, to make efforts to determine the military status of the child's parent or guardian. If DHR determines the parent or guardian is military, DHR must notify a U.S. Department of Defense Family Advocacy Program at the relevant military installation that an allegation is being investigated involving the military parent's or guardian's child.

DoD Family Advocacy Program materials describe FAP as a prevention-and-response system for child abuse/neglect and domestic abuse that coordinates with civilian agencies. In military child-abuse reporting, FAP may coordinate with command, law enforcement, medical treatment facilities, and civilian child-welfare services depending on the report and governing process.

This establishes a **civilian-state -> military-family-advocacy notification edge**.

It does **not** by itself establish:

- military jurisdiction over the Alabama custody case;
- a military order to influence a judge;
- a coordinated campaign against a parent;
- DARVO;
- criminal wrongdoing;
- psychological warfare.

### Counterweight: military-status protection

Alabama Code §30-3-9 provides that military deployment may not be used as the sole factor in an original or modified custody determination. The statute also recognizes SCRA-related stays/continuances.

So Alabama law contains both a military-notification bridge in child-welfare investigations and a custody protection limiting deployment-based adverse inference.

```text

MILITARY_STATUS != COURT_AUTHORITY
FAP_NOTIFICATION != CUSTODY_FINDING
FAP_NOTIFICATION != COMMAND_FINDING
```
